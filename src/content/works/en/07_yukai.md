## Why I built it

The 62nd Kosen Festival at Osaka Metropolitan University College of Technology was the last one to be held at the Neyagawa Campus. To mark the occasion, our class, 3I, decided to create a horror-themed VR escape game set in our own school.

## What I did

As the representative of our class exhibition, I was mainly responsible for campus coordination, promotional design, and exhibition operations.

Specifically, I obtained point cloud data of the campus, arranged to borrow VR headsets and PCs for the exhibition, secured permission from teachers to feature them in the game, prepared festival-related documents for the Student Association, designed promotional materials for the 3I exhibition, and created staff schedules and operation manuals for both the preparation period and the festival itself.

I could not participate in the game development because my PC did not have sufficient hardware capabilities. Therefore, the technologies listed above are those used in the Yukai project, not necessarily technologies I used myself.


I developed the Missing Person Discovery Sequence with plenty of flashy, dopamine-inducing effects inspired by recent trends. During the festival, we attached it to the backs of volunteer classmates who had posed as missing persons for Yukai Cam and had them walk around the campus to help promote the exhibition.


As the shift manager, I stayed at the exhibition venue throughout the day to cover understaffed shifts and handle unexpected situations. On the second day, I had to take the morning off because I was feeling unwell, but I returned in the afternoon to help supervise the exhibition.

In addition to the VR game area, we set up a separate space to display materials about Yukai and a collection of fan art. I also helped proofread parts of the official book exhibited there, focusing on terminology consistency, academic writing conventions, and other editorial details.

## Where I got stuck

I wrote the operation manual with as many possible situations in mind as I could, but unexpected problems still occurred during the festival.

We encountered technical issues with our custom-built reservation system and the VR headsets. The biggest problem was keeping the headsets charged.

After discussing the issue with the development team, we initially planned to charge the headsets whenever there were no visitors. However, thanks in part to the advertisements that the designer and I had created, we attracted far more visitors than expected, despite our exhibition being located in a relatively remote part of the campus. As a result, the headsets eventually ran out of battery.

In the end, we had to close the VR experience area for one hour on each of the two festival days to allow the headsets to recharge.

## What I learned

I learned that even when an operation manual seems complete, unexpected incidents are inevitable once an exhibition is running. It is important not only to prepare detailed procedures but also to remain flexible and respond to situations that the manual does not cover.

I also learned that the hardware capabilities of development PCs need to be considered as part of project management. In the Yukai project, game development was handled almost entirely by a single person, largely because the other members' PCs did not have sufficient hardware capabilities.

This experience taught me that project management is not just about assigning tasks and managing schedules, but also about understanding the technical limitations and resources available to the team.
